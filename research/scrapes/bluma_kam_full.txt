# 08 · Key Account Management — Land, Prove, Expand

Winning a city or a developer is the *start*, not the finish. The biggest, cheapest growth in this
business comes from **expanding inside accounts we've already won** — more sites, more services, and
multi-year frameworks. This document is the account-management layer that sits on top of the SDR
motion. Written for the Key Account Management (KAM) role.

> **Core idea:** an SDR books the first meeting; a KAM turns one signed district into a city-wide
> framework. Net revenue retention is the scoreboard.

---

## 1. The account lifecycle

```
LAND          PROVE             EXPAND              RENEW & ADVOCATE
first deal → calibrate the → more sites /      → multi-year framework +
(pilot)      value model     services / dept     reference + referrals
             on real data    (land-and-expand)
```

Each stage has an owner action and a metric:

| Stage | What the KAM does | Success metric |
|---|---|---|
| **Land** | Smooth handover from SDR/AE; agree a success plan with measurable goals | Signed pilot + mutual success plan |
| **Prove** | Instrument the install; report real m², CO₂, and € saved vs. the model | Calibrated impact report delivered |
| **Expand** | Map new stakeholders & sites; propose the next phase | Expansion deals / net revenue retention |
| **Renew** | QBRs, multi-year framework, secure the reference | Renewal rate + referenceable account |

---

## 2. Account planning

For every key account, maintain a living plan with:

- **Stakeholder map** — economic buyer, champion, technical evaluator, blockers, and end-users
  (e.g. for a city: Head of Sustainability, Procurement, Finance, the District Manager).
- **Whitespace map** — what they've bought vs. everything they *could* buy (other districts, other
  building types, maintenance contracts, ESG-reporting add-ons).
- **Success plan** — the outcomes the customer agreed to, the metrics, and the review cadence.
- **Risk register** — budget-cycle risk, champion-leaving risk, delivery risk, and the mitigation.

---

## 3. Land-and-expand by segment

| Segment | Land (foot in the door) | Expand (grow the account) |
|---|---|---|
| **B2G** | One district / a cluster of municipal buildings | Other districts → city-wide framework → neighbouring municipalities via reference |
| **B2B** | One flagship development or HQ | The wider portfolio → standard spec across new builds → maintenance contract |
| **Hospitality** | One café site | Owner's other locations → small-chain rollout → co-branded program |

The expansion engine is the **impact report**: once we can show *real* m², CO₂, and € saved on the
first install, the next-site conversation stops being a sales pitch and becomes a budgeting exercise.

---

## 4. The Quarterly Business Review (QBR)

A tight, repeatable QBR is the KAM's most important recurring motion:

1. **Outcomes to date** — real metrics from delivered installs vs. the original success plan.
2. **Model vs. actual** — how the calibrated numbers compare to the projections (builds trust).
3. **Benchmark** — how this account compares to peer cities/developers (gentle competitive pull).
4. **Roadmap** — the proposed next sites/services and the value they unlock.
5. **Asks** — the reference, the intro, the budget line for next cycle.

---

## 5. Renewals & expansion playbook

- **Start early.** Open the renewal/expansion conversation one full budget cycle ahead — public and
  enterprise buyers plan slowly.
- **Lead with realized value.** "Here's what you've actually saved and removed" beats any new pitch.
- **Tie to their cycle.** Align proposals to the city budget cycle or the developer's project pipeline.
- **Make the champion a hero.** Give them the metrics, slides, and story to defend the spend internally.
- **Engineer referrals.** A referenceable account in Ring 1 is the cheapest way to open Ring 2.

---

## 6. Health scoring & metrics the KAM owns

| Signal | Healthy | At-risk |
|---|---|---|
| Value realized vs. plan | On / ahead of plan | Behind, unreported |
| Stakeholder coverage | Multi-threaded | Single champion |
| Engagement | QBRs attended, responsive | Slipping meetings |
| Expansion | Whitespace in motion | Flat, no next step |

**Owned metrics:** Net Revenue Retention (NRR), logo retention, expansion revenue, renewal rate,
reference / advocacy count, and time-to-first-value.

> Interview talking point: **acquisition gets the logo; account management compounds it.** The
> impact model isn't just a sales tool — it's the renewal and expansion engine, because it turns
> every install into a measured, defensible reason to buy more.
